# Global Trends and Heterogeneity in Modified Adjusted Net Savings
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12987)

arXiv:2611.12987v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Modified adjusted net savings is a derived weak sustainability indicator obtained by subtracting education expenditure from adjusted net savings (ANS). Because ANS equals net national savings plus education expenditure minus natural-resource depletion and carbon dioxide damage, MANS isolates the savings and depletion components and is useful when education expenditure would otherwise dominate cross-country comparisons. This study documents the global distribution, long-run trends, and cross-country heterogeneity of MANS excluding particulate emission damage over 1990-2021. The accounting base is the World Bank World Development Indicators series NY.ADJ.SVNX.CD, and MANS is constructed by subtracting education expenditure from this series. We combine global maps, aggregate time series, country-level heatmaps, distributional statistics, and income group comparisons. The results show that global MANS has increased substantially in aggregate, but its evolution as a share of nominal GDP has been more volatile and uneven across countries. Per capita MANS and global MANS totals remain concentrated in high-income and large economies, whereas low-income and lower-middle-income groups display persistently lower and more volatile outcomes. Country rankings are highly persistent, and some populous countries exhibit pronounced fluctuations associated with natural-resource dependence and structural change. The study provides a reproducible descriptive benchmark for monitoring sustainable development and highlights the need to interpret MANS in current US dollars alongside income-group and country-specific contexts. The findings are relevant for macroeconomic policy, natural-capital accounting, and the design of sustainability-oriented investment strategies.
