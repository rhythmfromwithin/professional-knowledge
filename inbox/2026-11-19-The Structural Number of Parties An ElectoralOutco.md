# The Structural Number of Parties: An Electoral-Outcome-Independent Baseline for Comparing Party-System Scale
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24714)

arXiv:2611.24714v1 Announce Type: cross
Abstract: How many parties can a country structurally sustain? Standard measures such as the effective number of parties (ENP) cannot answer this question. They are descriptive and post hoc: they summarize competition only after an election, and they depend on electoral returns that are often missing, interrupted, or unreliable in many developing democracies, particularly in Africa. They also offer no common scale for comparing the size of party registers with realized electoral fragmentation. We introduce the Structural Number of Parties (SNP), an outcome-independent baseline derived from two universally available quantities: population size and territorial area. Building on the logic of the seat product model and the cube-root law of assembly size, the SNP gives an ex ante benchmark that can be computed for any country and year, including where electoral data are absent. Applied to African states, the SNP enables a structural gap diagnosis that distinguishes under-fragmented systems, marked by hegemonic-party dominance, from over-fragmented ones, marked by excessive atomization. The framework thus provides a transparent and reproducible tool to inform institutional reform and the rationalization of party systems across the continent.
