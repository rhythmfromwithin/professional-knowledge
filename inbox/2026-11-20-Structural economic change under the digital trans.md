# Structural economic change under the digital transformation
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26338)

arXiv:2611.26338v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Productivity improvements in digital industries could massively reshape the structure of production networks and drive significant labour reallocation across sectors. We examine these adjustments using a calibrated agent-based model of the global economy. We consider two scenarios with the same cumulative increase in digital-supplier productivity, which differ in how digital inputs in exposed industries are incorporated into production. The key difference is whether digital inputs enter as standard intermediate inputs (low embeddedness) or are combined directly with primary factors (high embeddedness). Across economies, both scenarios generate gains in gross output, household utility, and GDP, although value-added gains are more modest and unevenly distributed across sectors. High embeddedness generally amplifies production responses, particularly in industries highly exposed to digital inputs. Structurally, as digital industries become central in the production network and attract labour from other activities, other industries move further upstream and downstream. Persistent labour reallocation pressures also indicate potential unemployment risks where workers cannot move to expanding industries. These findings stress that, beyond productivity gains derived from advances in digital technologies, economic and social consequences depend on how digital inputs integrate into production and on workers' possibilities to adapt to structural change.
