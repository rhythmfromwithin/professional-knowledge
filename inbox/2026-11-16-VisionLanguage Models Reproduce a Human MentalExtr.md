# Vision-Language Models Reproduce a Human Mental-Extrapolation Bias in the Watanabe Illusion
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17741)

arXiv:2611.17741v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly used for visual judgments, yet whether they share human perceptual biases, and where in the model such biases arise, is unclear. We addressed this with the Watanabe Illusion, a stimulus not previously studied quantitatively: an oblique segment inside a circle whose extension geometrically hits the topmost of 11 dots, yet human observers (n = 131) reported a dot near the middle of the column. Five VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-32B and 7B, Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.5 Instant, Gemini 3.5 Flash), given the identical stimulus and wording, all deviated in the same direction. In the open-weight Qwen2.5-VL-32B, linear probes on the vision encoder recovered the line angle and the target dot six to eight times more accurately than its behavioral responses. A layer-wise logit lens showed that the correct answer dominates in the middle layers of the language model and is overwritten in late layers only under a visual-judgment frame. Naming the illusion, requesting suppression of prior knowledge, forcing a visual chain of thought, and 390 semantic preambles left the response center unchanged; only disclosing the correct answer moved it. VLMs may therefore carry human perceptual biases at the language-generation stage, where they resist declarative correction.
