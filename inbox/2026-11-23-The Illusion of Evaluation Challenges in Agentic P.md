# The Illusion of Evaluation: Challenges in Agentic Product Evaluation Practice
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26923)

arXiv:2611.26923v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Agentic products introduce risks that extend beyond those of their underlying models, arising from their tools, permissions, data access, safeguards, and operating conditions. Existing evaluation instruments provide useful evidence about model behavior and agents in defined harnesses, but their results do not necessarily transfer to deployed products or cover risks created by product configurations and interactions. Identifying and measuring such risks is especially difficult before deployment, when failure pathways, exposure, and outcome distributions may be poorly understood. This raises a fundamental question: what can evaluation evidence establish about the safety of a particular product, and what decisions can it legitimately support?
We call the illusion of evaluation the appearance of safety assurance when producing an evaluation result is treated as sufficient evidence for claims that extend beyond what was measured. Drawing on practitioner experience, prior research, and twelve illustrative product contexts, we identify six methodological challenges spanning product-risk identification, ecological and measurement validity, metric interpretation, outcome verification, evidence transfer, and evidentiary sufficiency. We propose a granular transparency-and-decision-relevance record that makes risks, system configurations, assumptions, uncertainty, and evidentiary limits explicit. The proposal is a reporting and reasoning aid, not proof of safety or a deployment-approval standard. We conclude with open questions on valid product-level measurements, evidence transfer, and decision sufficiency.
