# Earth-strength magnetic-field axis modulates non-spatial probabilistic decision-making in humans
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22386)

arXiv:2611.22386v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Magnetoreception has been primarily studied in orientation and navigation, but whether Earth-strength magnetic fields influence non-spatial decisions remains unclear. Evidence for human magnetic responses is limited, and the transduction mechanism is unknown. Using a Go stone selection paradigm in which near-zero geomagnetic field and Larmor-frequency conditions previously showed lower black stone rates, we tested whether non-spatial choice depends on magnetic-field geometry. Fasted men played in pairs under ten static two-seat configurations varied component-wise at near-constant north-seat intensity. The north-seat black stone rate was lower under the two configurations with horizontal fields transverse to the geomagnetic field than under the other eight, which did not differ detectably. The difference emerged promptly, without evidence of outcome-driven learning or configuration-dependent associations with the partner's preceding actions, and process analyses linked it to the joint parity-choice outcome without isolating either step. A Larmor-frequency difference observed over the ambient field was not detected over horizontal-only or vertical-only backgrounds, suggesting a potential magnetoresponsive axis, although the background difference was statistically unresolved. Frequency, light-spectrum, and posture analyses support a radical-pair mechanism as a leading working hypothesis. These findings associate the geomagnetic field axis with human non-spatial choice, providing a paradigm linking magnetic sensing to decision processes.
