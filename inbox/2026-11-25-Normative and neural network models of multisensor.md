# Normative and neural network models of multisensory integration
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32829)

arXiv:2611.32829v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Animals continuously detect scents, sights and sounds, which their brains combine into diverse multisensory streams. Multisensory integration accelerates learning, increases reaction speeds and helps to resolve sensory ambiguities. In studying this process, the field has developed a continuum of models; from concise mathematical equations, with a handful of variables, to artificial neural networks, with billions of parameters. In this review, we divide this spectrum into two classes: normative models start from a hypothesis about how multisensory signals should be integrated, while neural network models aim to emulate how circuits could integrate them. Together, these models demonstrate that observers can integrate cues in a statistically optimal fashion, and help to explain experimental findings, including the diversity of multisensory neuron response profiles and network architectures. In this review, we provide a didactic overview of multisensory models, complete with accessible equations and Python code, and compare their assumptions and explanatory power. We detail what has been learned from these models. Finally, we discuss how the ideas and insights from classical theoretical models can be reconciled with those from artificial neural networks, and how this convergence should shape future research.
