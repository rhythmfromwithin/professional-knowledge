# On Monotonic Hamming Sphere Landscapes
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28510)

arXiv:2611.28510v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Monotonic Hamming Sphere Landscapes (MHSL) are introduced: binary landscapes whose fitness is nonincreasing with Hamming distance from a unique global optimum, while points on the same Hamming sphere may be ordered arbitrarily. The class includes OneMax, every nondecreasing unitation function with a unique optimum, and positive linear functions with sufficiently balanced weights. The one-to-one MHSL, called Hamming sphere encodings (HSE), are counted in total and up to hypercube symmetry and shown to be a vanishing fraction of all landscapes. For the elitist (1+1) EA, every MHSL in which no two spheres share a fitness value has the optimization-time distribution of OneMax, which with known results makes these landscapes the easiest for mutation rates below one half. The same equivalence and optimality are proved for a two-member genetic algorithm with tournament selection and no crossover, whose hitting time on these landscapes reduces to a chain with quadratically many states. All 40,320 one-to-one three-bit landscapes are then evaluated for the (1+1) EA and the two-member genetic algorithm under eight mutation rates, three crossover operators and two selection schemes. Below one half, the easiest symmetry classes are all and only the HSE; above one half they are the hardest. Differences among HSE are small and stem from selection and from the hypercube symmetries respected by mutation and crossover. Targeted five-bit experiments likewise find their fastest landscapes within the HSE class.
