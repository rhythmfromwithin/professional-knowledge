# Beneath the VIX: Probability, Severity, and Uncertainty Shocks
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03849)

arXiv:2610.03849v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Implied stock-market volatility is widely used to identify uncertainty shocks and discipline models of their macroeconomic effects. This paper gives distributional content to the option-price variation underlying these exercises. A normalized at-the-money forward put price, the Downside Protection Premium (DPP), closely tracks the VIX and preserves the principal macroeconomic responses in the specifications examined. Its price and local strike slope separate risk-neutral shortfall probability from conditional severity. Severity accounts for almost all monthly premium comovement and 91.5 percent of the recession log premium. Conditional gains also rise substantially, while conditional losses remain larger in phase averages. Despite this severity dominance, quotations across strikes frequently exclude a common-scale explanation, conditional on the maintained forward and premium construction. Realized-volatility and future-volatility-news innovations both raise protection costs primarily through severity, yet produce different estimated employment responses in common impact-severity units; simultaneous significance depends on the inference formulation. Together, these findings supply joint empirical restrictions on the connection between uncertainty, option prices, and economic activity.
