# Puzzles, People, and Publications: Measuring Shared Significance in Crosswords from 1942 to 2024
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26677)

arXiv:2611.26677v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Crossword puzzles are cultural objects that encode shared significance. We use interpretable, large-scale computational methods to analyze how crossword constructors, editors, and publications have adopted and adapted language to convey significance. Focusing on approximately 2.4 million crossword clue/answer pairs published in The New York Times between 1942 and 2024, we find shifting significance and differing editorial practices to be quantitatively measurable. By computationally measuring shared significance over time in crossword puzzle production, we are able to better understand cultural changes in the United States through the 20th and early 21st centuries.
