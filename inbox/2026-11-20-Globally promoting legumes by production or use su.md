# Globally promoting legumes by production or use subsidies
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26168)

arXiv:2611.26168v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Despite consensus on the positive economic, environmental, and health effects of expanding legume production and consumption, the share of cropland devoted to them remains relatively small, and their share of human consumption is negligible compared with other protein sources such as meat. The presence of externalities associated with legume production and consumption gives room for policy interventions. In this context, we estimate the effects of global subsidies on legume production and consumption using CGEBox, an open-source and open-access Computable General Equilibrium modelling platform, with integrated accounting for land use, land cover, and energy, and considering agricultural and non-agricultural households. The analysis offers high agri-food detail and a special focus on Ghana and Germany, two countries with different agroecological and socioeconomic conditions facing multiple food system issues. Compared to production subsidies, we find that use subsidies are more effective at reducing consumer prices of legumes and stimulating household intake of macronutrients, without displacing consumption of other foods. In contrast, production subsidies are more effective at boosting domestic supply, but also stimulate their feed use. Furthermore, subsidies drive up crop land to the detriment of pasture land and forests. The impact of these subsidies also reveals profound regional disparities, influenced by patterns of land use and of production and consumption.
