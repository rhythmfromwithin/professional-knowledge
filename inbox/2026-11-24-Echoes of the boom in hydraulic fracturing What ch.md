# Echoes of the boom in hydraulic fracturing: What changes in Louisiana's energy complex reveal about global energy cycles
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28309)

arXiv:2611.28309v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: The expansion of hydraulic fracturing (i.e. fracking) led to global shifts in energy markets that were felt acutely along the US Gulf Coast and particularly in the state of Louisiana. This article presents a data-focused examination of trends in the energy industry, and shows how they led to potentially counterintuitive, negative impacts in Louisiana's economy. Most notably, hydraulic fracturing led to a massive expansion in oil and gas supplies that contributed to a significant decline in oil prices in late 2014, contributing to significant job losses. Louisiana's economic downturns were tied to global shifts in energy markets that had major geostrategic implications. More broadly, results indicate that large and long period cycles in the supply, demand and price of oil can have widespread impacts on job creation and loss. In states like Louisiana with a large and diversified energy complex, the impacts of these trends can be highly uneven. One impact in the 21st Century appears to be highly concentrated areas of industrial development within a broader pattern of regional economic decline. Furthermore, trends identified in this study suggest the potential for the emergence of major cycles in the gas market. Whereas natural gas was previously mostly transported by pipelines and sold through long-term contracts, the emergence of liquified natural gas creates the potential for more volatile gas market dynamics. The implications of gas market cycles, while not yet known, could be of major economic significance.
