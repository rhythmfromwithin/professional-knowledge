# Differential coupling drives Turing pattern formation in neural fields: a mechanism for Parkinson's disease and deep brain stimulation
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33602)

arXiv:2611.33602v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Deep brain stimulation (DBS) is an effective treatment for the motor symptoms of Parkinson's disease (PD), yet its mechanism of action remains incompletely understood. Abnormal beta-band synchronization in the basal ganglia and thalamocortical circuit is a hallmark of PD, but a theoretical framework linking network synchronization to spatial pattern formation is lacking. Here we show that a two-layer neural field model with differential interlayer coupling undergoes a Turing instability, spontaneously generating a diverse repertoire of spatially ordered patterns, including stripes, spots, rings, and spiral waves. The model yields the critical conditions and wavelengths for this instability, quantitatively predicting the observed pattern morphologies. Crucially, the interlayer difference, a natural measure of synchrony, exhibits strongly regime dependent dynamics. These findings establish a dynamical framework linking spatial pattern formation to pathological beta-band synchronization, providing a theoretical mechanism for DBS modulation of network states and a mathematical basis for optimizing stimulation parameters.
