# Human and Machine Annotations Predict Brain Responses to Naturalistic Movies Across Development
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13637)

arXiv:2611.13637v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Naturalistic movie watching provides a powerful framework for studying dynamic brain function, but linking movie content to neural responses typically relies on labor-intensive human annotation. We investigated whether multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can provide scalable socio-affective and semantic annotations for naturalistic fMRI. We compared MLLM-generated theory-of-mind (ToM) and valence ratings with human ratings and with regional BOLD responses across three animated movies. For two of these movies, we combined machine-derived visual features, natural-language scene descriptions, and socio-affective ratings in an ROI-wise encoding model of fMRI data from 66 participants aged 5--22 years. MLLM ratings partially tracked human judgments, but their neural correspondence differed by construct and model: ToM showed the most consistent human--machine overlap in visual cortex, whereas valence correspondence was largely movie dependent. In the encoding model, visual features yielded the strongest and most widespread prediction, whereas scene descriptions were more spatially selective, concentrated in dorsal-attention and visual regions and extending into right lateral temporal cortex. Age-group comparisons suggested feature-specific developmental differences: scene-description encoding in dorsal-attention cortex was higher in the oldest group, whereas visual-feature encoding in salience/ventral-attention, limbic, and control networks peaked in the middle group. Together, these findings indicate that MLLM-generated annotations capture meaningful aspects of human judgments and neural responses, supporting MLLMs as a scalable complement to, rather than a replacement for, human annotation in naturalistic neuroimaging.
