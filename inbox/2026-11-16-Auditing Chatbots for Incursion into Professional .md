# Auditing Chatbots for Incursion into Professional Legal Practice
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17622)

arXiv:2611.17622v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: The provision of legal advice has long been in the exclusive jurisdiction of licensed attorneys-a boundary that unauthorized practice of law (UPL) doctrine maintains to protect the public from incompetent counsel. We argue that this boundary is being redrawn, not by courts or bar authorities, but by frontier AI companies. While early chat models were trained to avoid dispensing legal advice, modern models have been reconfigured to reduce refusals and prioritize helpfulness. We ask whether chatbots increasingly do tasks that require a law degree and bar admission, and whether this is harmful in high-stakes legal domains. We develop UPLBench, a benchmark derived from court decisions finding UPL in high-stakes legal domains (e.g., immigration, family law). We then audit 23 chatbot models dating from 2023 to present, evaluating whether chatbots refuse or provide substantive legal advice, and whether they refer users to lawyers or legal aid. Unlike prior work that aims to establish the legal capabilities of chatbots (e.g., bar exam performance), our approach converts legal doctrine into a testable benchmark for evaluating chatbot adherence to law. We find modern chatbots increasingly perform tasks courts reserve for lawyers and decreasingly recommend that users consult lawyers. Subject matter expert attorneys reviewing our transcripts identified interaction patterns, such as failure to elicit relevant facts, that would be considered inadequate counsel. If acted upon, such advice could lead to deportations, loss of homes, and family separations-the very harms that UPL rules are designed to protect against. We weigh paths forward to protect the public, including compelling models to comply with existing law. We call upon the legal profession to respond to this incursion into its domain of expertise as there are insufficient protections and remedies for users who rely on chatbot legal advice.
