# Early Assessment Submission and Subsequent Course Outcome: A Day-30 Landmark Observational Analysis of OULAD with Explicit Weighting Diagnostics
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27005)

arXiv:2611.27005v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: We examined the association between submission of an assessment by day 30 and final course outcome in the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD). To avoid classifying students who withdrew during the exposure window as comparable non-submitters, the main analysis was restricted to students enrolled at a day-30 landmark. In the CCC/2014J course presentation, 1,820 students remained after exclusions for missing covariates and unsupported exposure categories; 1,624 had submitted at least one non-banked assessment by day 30. The difference in favorable final outcome rates (Pass or Distinction) was 0.3743. A normalized inverse-probability-weighted contrast was 0.3861 (95% full-refit bootstrap interval 0.3242 to 0.4447). The overlap-weighted contrast was 0.3261 (0.2667 to 0.3870), describing a different weighted population. The weights were unstable in the smaller comparison group, and measured covariates remained imbalanced under inverse-probability weighting. We independently reconstructed the cohort and estimates from the official UCI archive, examined eligibility and missing-data exclusions, and repeated descriptive analyses in three other course presentations. Those comparisons also showed positive associations, but their confidence intervals cannot account for unmeasured differences between the groups. The study identifies a reproducible association among selected learners, not the effect of requiring or encouraging earlier submissions.
