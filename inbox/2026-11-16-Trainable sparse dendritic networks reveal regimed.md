# Trainable sparse dendritic networks reveal regime-dependent roles for shunting inhibition and morphology
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17434)

arXiv:2611.17434v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Neural circuits must compute with noisy inputs and adapt their responses to context. How dendritic structure and synaptic integration support these functions remains unclear. We introduce DendriNet, a trainable rate-based framework with sparse excitatory and inhibitory contacts, controllable dendritic architecture and alternative integration rules. In image-classification tasks, shunting networks transmit and rely on distal dendritic signals more strongly than additive networks. Distributing limited contacts across compartments improves classification in small populations, whereas greater serial dendritic depth provides no benefit on clean images within resource-matched trees. Robustness depends on input statistics: deeper shunting trees better tolerate independent pixel noise, while addition better tolerates strong global scaling. In a contextual task with gain variation and noise, shunting improves accuracy under the training corruption but depends less on context-specific inhibition than a separately tuned additive network. Learning leak conductances partly restores shunting accuracy on dim, noise-free inputs outside the training distribution. At AlexNet width, shunting integration and learned sparse connectivity support smaller deployed networks with accuracy comparable to retrained dense layers. These results distinguish the local action of synaptic integration from the computational roles acquired by inhibitory pathways through learning, and suggest experiments on dendritic function and design principles for brain-inspired networks.
