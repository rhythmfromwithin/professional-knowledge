# Moving Beyond the Flat Screen: Opportunities and Challenges of Extended Reality for Studying Human Brain and Behavior
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19199)

arXiv:2610.19199v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Much of what we know about human perception and cognition comes from austere experimental settings: static stimuli presented to sedentary observers on flat monitors, complex responses reduced to simple button presses, and brain activity recorded from immobilized heads using EEG or fMRI. These highly controlled paradigms have provided remarkable insights, but capture a small and often unusual slice of behavior, raising questions about how well the findings generalize to the real world. Extended reality (XR), spanning both immersive virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) that overlays digital content on the real world, offers a way beyond these constraints, supporting controlled, reproducible experiments in which participants actively engage with three-dimensional tasks. Drawing on a growing body of VR and AR studies of attention, memory, and language -- our own and others', some involving EEG -- we outline what XR makes newly possible for cognitive neuroscience and reflect on the practical and conceptual obstacles that remain. The opportunities are substantial, but realizing them requires solving practical problems of hardware, timing, and measurement, and being realistic about what a virtual or augmented world can and cannot stand in for.
