# Cultural invariance in music-evoked emotions
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25189)

arXiv:2611.25189v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Worldwide, people produce and listen to music to induce, express, and regulate their own and others' emotions. To what extent culture influences the emotions evoked by music is, however, contested. The documented global variation in musical behaviours and emotion concepts suggests substantial cross-cultural differences in how listeners relate to music emotionally, while shared physiology and evolutionary history would predict broad global agreement on the emotional information extracted from music. Some evidence suggests shared processes but experiments on the subject do not represent global diversity in either music or participants. Here, we report a large citizen-science experiment wherein listeners (N = 124,394 in 189 countries; conducted in English) rated their emotional responses to popular Western music and to vocal music produced in mostly small-scale societies drawn from a globally representative corpus (i.e., songs assumed to be unfamiliar in style). We found strong similarity in listeners' emotional responses to music: Average agreement between pairs of countries' listeners was high. Furthermore, across both popular and unfamiliar types of music, emotional responses were shaped far more by individual listeners and the specific songs than by cultural background: Both between-person differences within a country and within-subject differences across songs explained substantially more variance in emotional responses than country-wise differences. Together, these results indicate that a listener's cultural background has only a marginal influence on the emotions they feel in response to music, raising the possibility of universal structure in emotional responses to music.
