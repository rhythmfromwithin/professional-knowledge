# Beyond What Is Visible: Context-Aware Sentiment Across Online Communities Among Crisis Events
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22551](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22551)

arXiv:2610.22551v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Social media offers timely insight into disaster experiences, yet online communities may not represent those affected on the ground. Uneven participation can bias crisis information systems toward data-rich locations and obscure communities with fewer digital traces. We address this with an adaptive cross-community learning framework that combines text and aggregate mobility signals with socioeconomic and hazard-informed community representations, supplementing sparse local observations with evidence from structurally related cities. We evaluate the framework on the January 2025 Southern California wildfires and 2024 Hurricane Helene in Florida. The adaptive model achieves the highest F1-score in both cases (0.5923 and 0.7615), while augmentation has its strongest effect where original observations are limited. The framework also reveals finer cross-city community-level sentiment variation and stronger sentiment-mobility correspondence than text-only models, with relationships differing across disaster contexts. These findings support crisis systems that better account for context-dependent evidence and improve interpretation of underrepresented community signals.
