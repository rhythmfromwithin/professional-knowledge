# Seabird-Aware Multi-Objective Offshore Wind Capacity Allocation: A Gippsland Case Study
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27073)

arXiv:2611.27073v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Offshore wind planning requires balancing capital cost, wind-resource potential, and seabird exposure. We formulate a spatial tri-objective mixed-integer linear program for offshore wind capacity allocation, minimizing capital cost and modeled seabird exposure while maximizing wind-weighted capacity. Applied to Australia's Gippsland offshore wind area, the model identifies exposure reductions of 23-44% at capital-cost premiums of 0.8-6.1% across 15 wind-weighted capacity requirements. We benchmark exposure-aware optimization against 19 percentile-based exclusion thresholds. At a fixed wind-weighted capacity requirement, the 5th-percentile threshold reduces exposure by 30.1% at a 37.6% cost premium, versus at least 30.1% at a 1.4% premium with optimization. Under a fixed capital budget, exclusion screening reduces exposure by 48.2% with a 26.9% capacity loss, compared with 48.5% and 7.6%, respectively, under optimization. These results highlight the value of explicitly optimizing graded seabird exposure.
