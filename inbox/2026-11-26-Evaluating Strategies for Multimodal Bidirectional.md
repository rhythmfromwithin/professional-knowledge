# Evaluating Strategies for Multimodal Bidirectional Interactions between Pedestrians and AVs
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33581)

arXiv:2611.33581v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) share spaces with Vulnerable Road Users (VRUs) in encounters where driver cues are absent. This can leave people uncertain whether the vehicle has noticed them and understood their request. Existing research has studied how AVs communicate with pedestrians through external human-machine interfaces. This work investigates bidirectional multimodal VRU-AV interactions combining Augmented Reality (AR), speech, and gestures. The study follows four stages: literature synthesis of interactions, a survey with 124 participants, interaction loop implementation, and feasibility evaluation. Participants preferred AR cues that made vehicle motion or warnings explicit, high-level voice commands, and conventional gestures. Prototype measurements revealed a trade-off between faster local processing and more accurate infrastructure-assisted processing. The demonstration shows bidirectional interaction using AR wearables and vehicular communications, while leaving decision-making to the AV. Together, the results support treating AV-VRU interaction as a closed loop of intent expression, recognition, vehicle decision, and feedback.
