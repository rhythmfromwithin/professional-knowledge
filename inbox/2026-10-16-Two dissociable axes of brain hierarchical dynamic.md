# Two dissociable axes of brain hierarchical dynamics track universal and population-specific effects of mindfulness
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20854)

arXiv:2610.20854v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: How mental training reshapes the temporal architecture of large-scale brain dynamics and how these mechanisms differ between healthy and clinically vulnerable populations remains poorly understood. Time-averaged neural measures collapse the multi-scale nature of brain activity, obscuring the dynamic processes through which behavioural interventions reorganise hierarchical brain function. Here, applying a sliding-window Temporal Irreversibility Index to source-reconstructed EEG across three complementary cohorts (n = 141), we decompose hierarchical brain trajectories into three orthogonal temporal descriptors and uncover two dissociable, mechanistically interpretable axes of intervention-induced plasticity. Cumulative meditation practice drives a universal, pan-cerebral reduction in the exploratory breadth of hierarchical dynamics, tracking dose-dependently with lifetime hours of practice. Temporal persistence is bidirectionally tuned by 8-week mindfulness-based interventions in a population-specific way: reduced in healthy meditators but increased in trauma-exposed individuals, revealing that comparable MBI protocols engage qualitatively distinct network mechanisms depending on baseline clinical status. A joint Hidden Markov Model further links these dynamical shifts to distinct microstate substrates, and longitudinal changes selectively track improvements in a single facet of mindfulness, attentional presence in daily life. Together, these findings establish a mechanistic framework for how behavioural training reorganises non-equilibrium brain dynamics, with direct implications for tailoring mindfulness-based interventions to individual clinical profiles.
