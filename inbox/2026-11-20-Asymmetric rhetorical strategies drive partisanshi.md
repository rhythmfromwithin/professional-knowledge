# Asymmetric rhetorical strategies drive partisanship in television news
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25140)

arXiv:2611.25140v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Television remains a dominant source of political information in the United States, yet systematic evidence on how partisanship is expressed in televised news remains limited. Prior research has largely treated partisanship as a one-dimensional construct, offering limited insight into the rhetorical strategies that underlie ideological bias. Here, by analyzing over 400,000 news episodes using large language models (LLMs), we provide a multi-dimensional measure of partisanship bias in television news across six major U.S. news stations (2013--2025). We identify eight interpretable linguistic features that explain 73% of partisanship variance in television news.\ Our findings reveal systematic asymmetries across stations. While MSNBC and CNN shifted leftward during Republican presidencies and moderated under Democratic presidencies, Fox News increased its conservative alignment following each election regardless of the electoral outcome. Beyond aggregate trends, MSNBC and CNN emphasized attacks on Republican actors more than reinforcement of Democratic positions, whereas Fox News reinforced Republican viewpoints substantially more than it attacked Democratic viewpoints. The association between in-group praise and out-group attacks was also threefold stronger on Fox News than on MSNBC. These results are validated against trained human raters and replicated with four additional LLMs with differing political leanings. Together, our study reveals systematic station-level differences in the rhetorical strategies with which ideological slant is communicated in television news, opening new opportunities for future research on media effects, audience attitudes, and political communication.
