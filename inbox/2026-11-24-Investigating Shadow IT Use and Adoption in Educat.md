# Investigating Shadow IT Use and Adoption in Educational Institutions and their Privacy Implications
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28280)

arXiv:2611.28280v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Digital technologies have become ubiquitous in education, integrated to nearly all aspects of teaching, learning, research, and communication. They collect enormous amounts of data from their users, primarily students. When procured institutionally, data collection and use are guided by legally binding contracts. However, recent studies suggest that apps found on the open web are increasingly being used for teaching and evaluation without going through a formal audit, raising concerns about privacy, security, and regulatory compliance. How prevalent such shadow IT use in education is, and what privacy and compliance risks they create remain unexplored. We address this gap through a sequential survey-based study: one survey with 375 educators and another with 21 system administrators at K-12 or HEIs (Higher Education Institutes). We discover widespread use of external apps by educators: 1,373 apps were reported, 494 among them were unique, primarily used for pedagogical utility (n=213) and functional convenience (n=155). Their adoption and integration are often facilitated, even encouraged, by administrators; this result deviates from studies in other domains (e.g., industry) where admins actively prevent them. Many educators (n=62) continued using apps they do not trust, citing a lack of alternatives or dependency as barriers to discontinuation. We also found that fewer than one in three educators were aware of institutional policies on shadow IT use (K-12: 30.3%, HEI: 24.8%), and 22 knowingly violated such policies. While 107 received formal warnings for using shadow IT, only 33 adjusted their behavior. We conclude by discussing the implications of our findings and future recommendations for various stakeholders to minimize the risks.
