# Burning the Bridge: Agentification and Domestic Value Added
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31518)

arXiv:2611.31518v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Firms that replace local work with foreign AI agents can produce more per paid hour while generating less income at home. We study this trade-off in a small open economy where exporters and local services both use agents. Once wages reach a reservation floor, extending agent use in local services lowers domestic income at unchanged foreign costs. Value added at constant prices and consumption also fall if suppliers capture enough of the saving. Exporters have an offset: adopting agents can expand production and earn the foreign exchange needed to pay for them. The difference is substantial. Using Brazilian accounts as a reference, a supplier bargaining share above about 0.15 is sufficient to make further local-service adoption reduce value added even at negligible foreign costs. The corresponding threshold in tradables is about 0.66. We derive separate export-growth requirements for preserving income and consumption and illustrate them using historical accounts for Brazil and Mexico. Cheaper agents ease the consumption requirement; lower export prices raise it. Over time, declining domestic capabilities can also make returning to local production more expensive, allowing suppliers to charge more at renewal.
