# Regime-dependent reconstruction of structural connectivity: an in silico study of spatially constrained modular neuronal networks
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25830)

arXiv:2611.25830v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Inferring structural connectivity from neuronal activity is a major challenge in neuroscience, yet the conditions under which inferred interactions faithfully reflect anatomical connections remain unclear. Here, we investigate in silico how spatial constraints in a neuronal network and its dynamical regime jointly shape structural recoverability. We construct modular networks with varying average axonal lengths and simulate spontaneous activity using the Izhikevich model with synaptic depression across a broad range of excitatory strengths and background noise levels. Using Generalized Transfer Entropy, we infer effective connectivity and compare it with the underlying structural ground-truth at the levels of individual connections and global topology. We observe that the richness in the repertoire of activity patterns is highest in an intermediate regime between globally synchronized and noise-dominated activity, reflecting a balance between global excitation and stochasticity. Optimal structural reconstruction generally occurs under these same conditions, although this correspondence depends on network architecture and is weaker in highly modular networks. Together, these results show that structural recoverability depends jointly on network organization and dynamical regime. More broadly, our findings highlight the risks of treating inferred effective connectivity as a direct surrogate for structural connectivity.
