# Clicks, Carbon, and Risk: Climate Attention, Persistent Financial Risk, and Real-Sector Activity
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22517)

arXiv:2611.22517v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Do shifts in public climate concern alter persistent financial risk and subsequently matter for real activity? We combine daily U.S. sectoral equity returns with a monthly Climate Concern Index (CCI) constructed from internet search intensity and decomposed into adverse and transition-oriented components. GARCH-MIDAS estimates reveal substantial heterogeneity: adverse climate concern raises persistent volatility in carbon-intensive Energy activities, while transition-oriented attention tends to reduce persistent volatility in regulated Utilities, with important differences across sub-sectors. We then construct a persistent-risk differential between Energy and Utilities and examine its predictive content for sectoral real activity using local projections. Innovations associated with adverse climate concern precede declines in Oil and Gas industrial production and employment over the following quarters, while comparable Utilities responses are limited. The production result is preserved when the financial predictor is reconstructed using the more closely matched Oil, Gas, Coal equity sub-sector and remains economically similar across several robustness checks. Overall, the findings establish a link between climate concern, persistent sectoral financial risk, and subsequent real-sector activity.
