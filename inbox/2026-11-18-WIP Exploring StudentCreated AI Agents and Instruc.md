# WIP: Exploring Student-Created AI Agents and Instructional Videos to Foster Engagement in Computing Education
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23058)

arXiv:2611.23058v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: This work-in-progress (WIP) research-to-practice study examines the use of student-created artifacts to promote active learning in a large undergraduate Software Design and Engineering (SWE) course. Active learning strategies have been shown to significantly enhance student engagement, comprehension, and motivation, particularly in computing education. In this work, we explore the use of student-created artifacts to foster student engagement in higher-level Computer Science (CS) courses. Specifically, we examine the experiences of undergraduate and graduate CS students who participated in the creation of two types of artifacts: instructional video tutorials and conversational AI agents. The goal being to understand how designing these artifacts could support students in applying and learning course content. Students raised valuable insights regarding both experiences. Concerning the creation of instructional videos, they appreciated the interactivity brought through multimedia to their learning experiences, although some found the strategy to be time-consuming. Similarly, when designing conversational AI agents, students enjoyed the novelty and practical application of the task, while some encountered challenges related to the complexity of tools such as \textit{Google Dialogflow ES}. We report on the potential of student-created artifacts to foster deeper engagement with course material, encouraging both creativity and critical thinking in computing education.
