# Characterizing Open-Source Video Games from a Software Engineering Perspective
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03953)

arXiv:2610.03953v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Video games hold a prominent role in the entertainment industry, which constitutes a multibillion-dollar global market. Within this ecosystem, independent studios, small teams, and solo developers operate with constrained budgets while making their games available through platforms such as itch.io, where many projects are also available as open-source. Where even if the project is fully released, other developers can still have access to the development process through the source code, helping other developers to develop their own games. These open-source games also constitute a valuable corpus for empirical software engineering research, offering transparent access to development artifacts that are typically inaccessible in commercial projects. This study characterizes open-source video games from a Software Engineering standpoint, focusing on their genres, popularity, repository activity, implementation characteristics, and testing practices. To this end, we extracted metadata from 491 video games from itch.io, published as open-source projects in GitHub. The results indicate that open-source games predominantly fall within limited genres (Puzzle, Action, and Platformer), that player ratings, when available, tend to be high, bringing a perception that the community receives well those kind of games, that repositories are largely dormant or minimally maintained, that the majority of these projects adopt either Godot or Unity as their game engine, and that documentation of testing-related practices is seldom included. The results offer insights into the development of open-source video games and may contribute to advancing software engineering practices and tools designed for small teams and independent game developers.
