# Evolutionary-based Transfer Learning for Physical Attack on End-to-End Autoencoder Communications
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17393)

arXiv:2611.17393v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: End-to-end autoencoder communication has emerged as a promising means of achieving efficient data transmission across various domains. Nevertheless, vulnerabilities have recently been revealed in these systems by adversarial attacks, wherein malevolent entities manipulate the transmitted signals to deceive the autoencoder. This paper leverages the applications of evolutionary algorithms to solve a challenging optimization problem of launching physical attacks against end-to-end autoencoder communication. For white-box scenarios, we introduce PAGA (Physical Attack based on Genetic Algorithm), which combines a genetic algorithm with elite population initialization to craft attack signals that degrade autoencoder performance. To gauge the efficacy of these attack signals, we employ a fitness function that assesses their capacity to induce substantial reconstruction errors and misclassification rates. A Multi-Factorial version of PAGA, referred to as PAMFGA, is proposed to deal with practical scenarios where the attackers have limited knowledge about the receiver, also known as a black-box attack. Our experimental results show that the proposed attacks can substantially degrade the reliability of the considered autoencoder communication systems across the evaluated settings. This study also underscores the critical importance of incorporating security considerations into the design and implementation of end-to-end autoencoder communication systems.
