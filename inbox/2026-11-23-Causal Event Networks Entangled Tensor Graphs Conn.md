# Causal Event Networks: Entangled Tensor Graphs, Connectome Quotient Topology, and Subjective Experience as Geometric Dual
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26646)

arXiv:2611.26646v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: We introduce a chromatically constrained tensor-product construction that generates sparse recurrent computational graphs whose coloring bounds the number of sequential execution phases: vertices are computational units, edges are causal message-passing channels, and the graph can grow arbitrarily large while its chromatic causal depth stays fixed. Coarsened to the scale of the Budapest Reference Connectome, the community quotient of a simple local construction reproduces the connectome's broad degree profile, passes a nine-criterion topological test and comes close to the connectome on most of a further battery of complexity, spectral, controllability and clique measures, while the fine parent graph shows a scale separation: multiscale organization is intrinsic to the fine graph, and short-path integration appears only under coarsening. Placing short-path integration directly in the fine substrate, with chromatically admissible bridges and targeted sparsification, yields quotients far less modular than the connectome across a wide range of coarsening schemes. Relocating edges into the interiors of quotient nodes instead produces a quotient that matches the connectome's degree profile, edge count and fiber-bundle distribution, passes the full nine-criterion test and comes close on most complexity and spectral measures; its fine substrate fails only the two near-random path criteria. Used as the deep-feature pathway of a U-Net encoder-decoder, the graph-derived recurrent module improves image inpainting over the convolutional baseline. We close with a conjectural, structural account of subjective experience in holographic terms: the graph acts as an evolution operator that fixes the admissible causal order and the geometries the substrate can support, and experience occupies the position of the bulk dual of a particular activity state.
