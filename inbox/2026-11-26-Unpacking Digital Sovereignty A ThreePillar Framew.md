# Unpacking Digital Sovereignty: A Three-Pillar Framework and a Research Agenda
> 原文: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33397)

arXiv:2611.33397v1 Announce Type: new
Abstract: Digital sovereignty has moved rapidly from policy jargon to global discourse, yet it lacks a solid conceptual foundation. Building on research from other disciplines, we propose a three-pillar framework (governance, infrastructure, and practice) to help IS scholars unpack how sovereignty is enacted, stabilized, or undermined across pillars. We argue that the most fruitful analytical work emerges at the intersections among the three pillars, where IS research is particularly well positioned to contribute. We introduce a local-distal axis that positions actions across scales of scope and proximity, and the notion of anchors, i.e., critical phenomena that cut across pillars and make the framework analytically tractable. We illustrate the framework using data as an anchor, showing how digital sovereignty depends on the alignment of governance, infrastructure, and everyday practice, and is undermined where they diverge. We close with a research agenda for IS scholars studying digital sovereignty empirically.
